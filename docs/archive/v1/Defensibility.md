# EMMA: Will it last?

The five ways startups die, applied honestly to EMMA, and what EMMA must do best to survive. Written 2026-10-14.

## The one-line answer
EMMA is not "AI that writes posts". That is a feature, and ChatGPT, Meta AI and Canva already ship it. EMMA survives only if it becomes **the place a small Kenyan business runs its marketing**: its calendar, leads, follow-ups and results, with a person-quality plan delivered for KES 500, and with a local agency (LionsLane) behind it.

## 1. Feature pretending to be a company? **Our biggest risk.**
- **The threat:** Meta can put "write me a post" inside WhatsApp Business tomorrow, and ChatGPT already writes captions for free. If EMMA's value is the text, we're competing with a button.
- **What the platforms can't easily copy:**
  - **Workflow lock-in.** The calendar, the leads pipeline, the approvals and the history live in EMMA. After three months, leaving means losing your marketing memory. A chat app forgets you every time.
  - **Distribution they don't have:** LionsLane's client book, referrals and SME groups, sold in shillings over M-Pesa by people who answer the phone.
  - **Local data that compounds:** which posts, offers and follow-ups actually work for Kenyan salons, cafés and clinics (anonymised and aggregated). Big platforms don't model "Mashujaa Day promo for a Westlands salon".
  - **Brand and trust:** a named local team, the Kenya Data Protection Act built in, and nothing posting without your approval.
- **So the product must:** make the workspace the system of record (calendar, leads, results), not the chat window. Every feature that only generates text is weak; every feature that remembers, tracks or follows up is strong.

## 2. Founders who don't understand the industry? **Our strength.**
LionsLane runs marketing for real Kenyan businesses (TriMart, Maji Amare, Ovanzi, Tinganga and others). We know the real problems aren't "writing": owners don't know what to post, stop after a week, and lose WhatsApp enquiries that ask the price and go quiet. EMMA should be built from those client playbooks, not from generic marketing theory.

## 3. Marketplace with no wedge? **Not applicable, keep it that way.**
EMMA is single-sided software. If we ever connect businesses to freelance designers or strategists, start with one wedge: one city (Nairobi), one category (salons or restaurants), LionsLane strategists as the first supply.

## 4. Hardware economics? **Not applicable, but AI cost is our version of it.**
Each brief costs real money, so we:
- price per plan by model (Haiku, then Sonnet, then Opus) and cap AI cost per user per month;
- measure cost per user from day one (`usage_events`);
- keep gross margin above 50% on paid plans, and revisit the limits after two weeks of real data.

## 5. Demand that only exists because we push? **Measure this, don't assume.**
The founding customers will come from LionsLane pushing. That's fine for month one, but it isn't validation. The signals that the market is *pulling*:
- **Week-4 retention ≥ 25%** without reminders from us.
- **Organic sign-ups** (no referral code, no ad) rising month on month.
- **Referrals:** users inviting other businesses.
- **Usage without prompting:** briefs on days we didn't send a reminder.

If these stay flat when we stop pushing, the problem is the product or the buyer, not the marketing.

## What gets stronger as EMMA grows
| As we add… | It gets… |
|---|---|
| More businesses per sector | Better sector playbooks: proven post angles, offers and follow-up timing for that kind of business |
| More months per business | Harder to leave: history, leads, results and brand voice live in EMMA |
| More LionsLane clients | Cheaper distribution: each client's network becomes a referral channel |
| More approvals and rejections | A sharper EMMA: every approve or discard teaches what this business accepts |

## What EMMA must do best (in this order)
1. **Never let a lead go cold.** Track every enquiry and draft the next WhatsApp/email follow-up on time. That is money owners can see.
2. **Always know what to post.** A calendar built around their dates, offers and customers, refilled every week.
3. **Prove it worked.** Leads, sales and spend in one place, in shillings.
4. **Stay in their voice and the law.** Brand voice, consent flags, approval before anything goes out.

## Product changes this implies (roadmap)
- Lead follow-up reminders and a "who to call today" list (v1.1, top priority).
- Weekly calendar refill brief, triggered automatically (v1.1).
- Results logging in two taps on mobile ("got a sale from this post") to build the outcome data (v1.1).
- Anonymised sector playbooks fed back into EMMA's planning (v2, with consent and privacy review).
